Your home's heat lossStarting
Heat loss
—
What's this?
The watts it takes to keep your home each 1 °C warmer than outside: at 10 °C warmer inside, 10 times this. It's the slope of the line on the chart below (heat a day against inside − outside). Lower is better insulated. ± is how sure it is (95%): it narrows with more days, and most with days at quite different temperatures.
At design temperature
—
What's this?
The heat your home needs on a very cold day: to stay at the design inside temperature when it's the design outside temperature (Settings › Design temperatures). It's what a heat pump has to supply, and what your surveys quote: the heat loss × the difference between the two. W/m² is the same per square metre of floor.
Hot water
—
What's this?
Where the line on the chart crosses ΔT 0: hot water and cooking, less the free heat from the sun, people and appliances. It doesn't change the heat loss.
Boiler efficiency
—
What's this?
How much of the gas becomes heat in your home. A condensing boiler is about 97% at 35 °C flow, falling to about 87% at 70 °C: cooler water lets it condense more of the heat out of its exhaust. From your flow temperature (Settings › Flow temperature), or the efficiency you set (Settings › Your boiler). Gas × this is the boiler's heat; other heating's is added to it.
Fit
—
What's R²?
How closely the days sit on the line, from 0 to 1. 1: every day exactly on it; 0: no pattern at all. Over 0.85 the heat loss is well measured. Lower means the days scatter (other heat such as a wood burner or lots of sun, a day away, the house warming up after a setback), so leave odd days out on the Days tab, or wait for more. The ± on the heat loss says the same in watts.

How it's worked out
  1. Each day (midnight to midnight) is read from Home Assistant's history once it's over (today so far on every refresh): the gas used (from the changes in your gas sensor, which starts again at midnight), and the average outside, inside and flow temperatures. Inside is your floors' thermostats averaged by floor area.
  2. Other heating (Settings › Other heating), e.g. air con units: the electricity they use × their COP (the heat out for each kWh in) is added to the boiler's heat.
  3. Gas to heat: each half hour's gas times the boiler's efficiency at the flow temperature: a condensing boiler is about 97% at 35 °C, falling to about 87% at 70 °C, as less of its exhaust condenses.
  4. The fit: a straight line through each day's heat against inside − outside (ΔT). Its slope ÷ 24 hours is the heat loss in W/K: the watts it takes to keep the house 1 °C warmer than outside. Today so far joins in: its heat is scaled up to a whole day and it pulls on the line by the part of the day that has passed (a quarter of a day counts a quarter), so a cold morning helps straight away without counting as a whole day.
  5. Hot water: on a combi, gas burnt while none of your thermostats is calling for heat is hot water (showers, taps), not heat for the house, so it's taken out (Settings › Hot water). Showers while the heating's on can't be told apart; they're about the same every day, so they don't change the slope, just where the line crosses ΔT 0 (with the free heat from the sun, people and appliances).
  6. Left out: days less than the smallest ΔT warmer inside (the heating's barely on), days with gaps in the readings, and days you leave out on the Days tab (e.g. away, or the oven on all day).
  7. R² (the Fit tile) is how closely the days sit on the line, from 0 (no pattern) to 1 (all exactly on it): over 0.85 is good. Days well off the line usually had something else going on (other heat, away, a big change to the thermostats); leave them out on the Days tab.
  8. Design heat loss: the heat loss × (design inside − design outside), e.g. 21 °C and −3 °C: what a heat pump survey quotes. ± is how sure the fit is (95%): more days, and colder ones, narrow it.

Anything else that heats your home, e.g. air con units or electric heaters: its energy sensor (kWh) and its COP, the heat out for each kWh in (about 3 to 4 for air con heating, 1 for a plain electric heater). Its heat is added to the boiler's.
